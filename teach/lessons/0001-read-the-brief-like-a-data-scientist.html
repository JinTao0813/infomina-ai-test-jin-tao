The assignment asks for a per-user location entropy function, analysis, findings, and product ideas. But its “what we are looking for” section is the real rubric: theoretical, technical, iteration, and creative capacity.

TheoryDefine probability, entropy, units, normalization, and limits.
TechnicalValidated loader, generalized API, tests, reproducibility.
IterationP0/P1/P2 scope; end-to-end result before optional depth.
CreativeTranslate evidence into a testable product concept.
JudgmentReject causal, cultural, and privacy-unsafe claims.

The repository mirrors that chain: data.py establishes trusted events, entropy.py owns the metric, tests define contracts, and the executed notebook owns evidence and interpretation. The generated website presents that notebook, while Discovery Mode makes one product hypothesis inspectable without pretending it is validated.

“I treated the task as an end-to-end analytical product exercise. The formula was necessary, but the evaluation also required trustworthy data, reusable code, defensible analysis, and a product hypothesis that did not outrun the evidence.”

Why the implementation order matters

The work forms vertical slices: first a correct loader, metric, tests, and executed analysis; then deeper temporal/category evidence; then the product argument; finally a bounded deterministic prototype and generated case-study site. That order protects the required answer before optional engineering range. If time had run out, the notebook still answered the assignment.

Why isn't a notebook-only entropy calculation enough?
It would show a formula but weak evidence of production adaptability: responsibilities would be coupled, contracts untested, validation implicit, and reuse difficult.

Clarify the metric, validate and clean source events, compute tested per-user entropy, analyze robustly, interpret cautiously, and propose a guarded experiment.

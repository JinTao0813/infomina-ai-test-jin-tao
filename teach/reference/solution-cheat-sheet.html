A possible two-minute answer

The brief asked for per-user location entropy, methodology and findings, and product ideas. I could not access the suggested EPFL data, so I used public Foursquare TSMC 2014 check-ins for NYC and Tokyo.

I built a strict dataset-specific loader and a separate reusable three-argument pandas metric. The loader validates schema, fields, coordinates, and time; removes only exact duplicates; keys users by city; and derives local context with named timezones. The metric counts each user's events by venue or category, computes base-2 Shannon entropy, and normalizes by log₂ of the observed support. Known-answer and edge-case tests establish the contract.

The city samples have nearly identical median normalized venue entropy—0.853 NYC and 0.860 Tokyo—with larger variation within cities. Most sufficiently observed users have higher weekend entropy, and category entropy shows that visiting many places is not identical to doing many kinds of activity. These are descriptive voluntary check-in patterns, not complete mobility or causal effects.

Those findings motivate Context-Aware Discovery. I implemented a deterministic full-stack prototype where explicit intent dominates bounded profile/context signals and transparently reranks a privacy-safe historical candidate catalog. Four profiles are synthetic; sixteen candidates are filtered, rounded, pseudonymous aggregates. A generated website presents the authoritative executed notebook without needing the raw data or API.

The prototype proves the hypothesis can be made inspectable; it does not prove recommendation quality. Validation needs a user-level randomized experiment on current consented data, with save or selection rate plus experience, distance, latency, sparse-history, fairness, privacy, and trust guardrails.

Five high-value defenses

  1. Why exact deduplication? Identical event replays otherwise gain extra probability mass; user-venue deduplication would erase real repeat behavior.
  2. Why composite users? 3,376 true city-user keys collapse to 2,293 bare IDs; all 1,083 NYC IDs overlap Tokyo's numeric space.
  3. Why static artifact? One notebook source of truth remains readable without raw files/API; hash and re-export checks reject staleness.
  4. Why deterministic ranker? Inspectable behavior and bounded scope without pretending a model has been trained or validated.
  5. Why pseudonymous candidates? Source venue names do not exist; city/category/number labels avoid fabrication and source-ID exposure.

Five honest weaknesses

  • Voluntary old check-ins are selected, incomplete, and not current population behavior.
  • The 20-per-context rule lacks history-length/threshold sensitivity analysis.
  • Cohort composition is check-in-weighted rather than equal-user weighted.
  • Confidence, rank weights, sparse threshold, and power curve are hand-designed.
  • Popularity-based baseline is not personal relevance; no outcome experiment or privacy review exists.

If you had another day

First quantify entropy stability and threshold sensitivity, then add equal-user composition. For product work, specify confidence estimation, current-data/privacy requirements, and power—not a fancier model. Add CI and raw-file hash assertions after the highest validity risks.

Never blur these: profile entropy ≠ candidate novelty ≠ category familiarity. Synthetic profiles ≠ data-derived pseudonymous candidates. Implementation correctness ≠ recommendation effectiveness.
